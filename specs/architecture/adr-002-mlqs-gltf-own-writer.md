# ADR-002-MLQS: Own stdlib glTF writer with procedural material parameters in extras

## Context

The viewer needs the Blueprint's geometry, the per-part `rnd` value and the procedural material
parameters. Blender does two jobs for us today that a plain export must do itself: it
triangulates concave n-gons and orients the faces outward.

## Decision

- `code/export_gltf.py` writes the `.glb` with the standard library only (`struct`, `json`) and
  runs with the system `python3`. `code/mesh_ops.py` triangulates (ear clipping) and orients each
  Part (consistent winding, positive signed volume). Open Parts are exported double-sided.
- Per object: one node, one primitive per material, attributes `POSITION`, `NORMAL`, `_RND`.
- Materials are `pbrMetallicRoughness` approximations. The full `MAT_DEF` entry and the paint
  override go into `extras`, and the viewer builds its own materials from them.
- `scene.extras` carries the room lights, key constants and the garden layout.
- Axes: z-up → y-up as `(x, z, −y)`, as Blender's exporter does.
- `web/public/frechenlehen.glb` is generated by `npm run blueprint` and git-ignored.

## Alternatives considered

- **Blender's glTF exporter.** It needs Blender in CI and drops the node materials.
- **`pygltflib`/`trimesh`.** An extra dependency for a few hundred lines of simple writing.
- **JSON instead of glTF.** It doesn't open in other glTF viewers, and we would need our own
  loader.

## Consequences

- Any glTF viewer shows the house in plausible colours.
- The `_RND` → `_rnd` attribute name is pinned by a test against the real GLTFLoader.
